# Ayodhya Circle Rates Explained: What Changed and What It Costs You

If you're buying land in Ayodhya, the circle rate is one number you can't ignore. It decides the minimum value at which your property can be registered, and that decides how much stamp duty you pay.

Ayodhya's circle rates went through a big revision in June 2025, the first in about eight years. Some areas saw rates triple. This guide explains what changed, what it means for your budget, and how to check the rate for any plot you're considering.

## First, what is a circle rate?

A circle rate is the minimum value per square metre (or per hectare, for agricultural land) at which property can be registered in a particular area. It's set by the district administration.

It's used for three main things:

- Calculating **stamp duty** and registration charges
- Setting a **floor value** so property can't be registered below it
- Deciding **compensation** when the government acquires land

The important bit: stamp duty is charged on **whichever is higher**, the actual sale price or the circle rate value. So even if you negotiate a great deal, you can't pay stamp duty on less than the circle rate.

## What changed in Ayodhya?

The revision took effect in June 2025. Before that, Ayodhya's rates hadn't been revised since 2017, even though land prices had shot up after the Supreme Court verdict and the start of temple construction.

How big was the gap? Officials had noted that over the three years before the revision, properties near the Ram Mandir area were selling at anywhere from 41% to over 1,200% above the circle rate. The old rates simply didn't reflect reality anymore.

Here's what the revision did:

- **Overall increase:** 30% to 200%, depending on location and land use
- **Within about 10 km of Ram Janmabhoomi:** increases of more than 150%
- **Around the temple:** ₹26,600 to ₹27,900 per sq m, up from ₹6,650 to ₹6,975

Some examples for agricultural land (per hectare):

| Village | Old rate | New rate |
|---|---|---|
| Tihura Manjha | ₹11 to 23 lakh | ₹33 to 69 lakh |
| Tihura Uparhar | ₹32 to 71 lakh | ₹42 to 95 lakh |
| Shahnawazpur Majha and Barahta Majha | ₹75 to 169 lakh | ₹98 to 221 lakh |

Areas with the most active land transactions saw the steepest hikes, which makes sense. The administration was trying to bring official values closer to what people were actually paying.

## What does this mean for your budget?

Let's take a simple example. Say you're buying a 100 sq m plot in a locality where the circle rate is ₹26,600 per sq m.

- Circle rate value: 100 × ₹26,600 = **₹26,60,000**
- Stamp duty at 7%: roughly **₹1,86,200**
- Registration fee (around 1%): roughly **₹26,600**

So before you've even started building, you're looking at over ₹2 lakh in government charges on that plot. Under the old rate, the same calculation would have been a fraction of that.

Women buyers in UP get a concession on stamp duty up to a certain property value. The limit has changed over the years, so check the current rule on igrsup.gov.in before you budget.

## Is the higher circle rate bad for buyers?

Not entirely. It's a mixed bag.

**The downside** is obvious: higher stamp duty and registration costs.

**The upside** is less talked about. Realistic circle rates make it harder to do "number one and number two" deals, where part of the payment is shown and part is paid in cash. That's good for transparency. It also helps landowners get better loan valuations, and it means fairer compensation if land is ever acquired.

And in some parts of Ayodhya, the new circle rate now sits close to or even above what sellers are asking. In those spots, the circle rate effectively becomes your price floor.

## How to check the circle rate for any plot

The official source is the UP Stamps and Registration Department portal, **igrsup.gov.in**. You can look up the circle rate by district, tehsil and village or locality. You can also ask at the sub-registrar's office (for Ayodhya city, that's usually the Sadar tehsil office).

Always check the rate for the exact village or mohalla, and for the right land type (residential, commercial or agricultural). Rates can vary a lot even within a few kilometres.

## A few more costs to keep in mind

If you're buying agricultural land and plan to build, you'll need a declaration for non-agricultural use under section 80 of the UP Revenue Code. The fee for that is linked to the circle rate (2% of the circle rate value is the commonly cited figure), so a higher circle rate pushes this cost up too.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the circle rate near Ram Mandir in Ayodhya?

After the June 2025 revision, circle rates in the area around the temple were set at ₹26,600 to ₹27,900 per sq m, up from ₹6,650 to ₹6,975.

### How much did Ayodhya circle rates increase?

Between 30% and 200% depending on location and land use. Areas within about 10 km of Ram Janmabhoomi saw increases of more than 150%.

### Where can I check the official circle rate for a plot in Ayodhya?

On the UP Stamps and Registration Department portal, igrsup.gov.in, or at the local sub-registrar's office.

### Is stamp duty charged on the circle rate or the sale price?

On whichever is higher. If your deal value is below the circle rate value, stamp duty is charged on the circle rate value.
