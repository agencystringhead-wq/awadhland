# GDA Gurukul City: What Gorakhpur's New 400-Acre Township Means for Land Buyers

Gorakhpur has been growing fast for a few years now, and the Gorakhpur Development Authority (GDA) clearly wants to stay ahead of it. Its latest move is **Gurukul City**, a proposed residential township of around 400 acres that will be part of the bigger **New Gorakhpur** development plan.

Here's what we know so far, and what it could mean if you're thinking of buying land in Gorakhpur.

## Where exactly is Gurukul City?

Gurukul City is planned along the **Sonauli Road corridor**, the road that runs north from Gorakhpur towards the Nepal border at Sonauli. The township will cover areas including **Maniram, Balapar and Rahmatnagar**.

If you know Gorakhpur, you'll know this side of the city has been picking up for a while. It's one of the directions the city is naturally expanding in, and GDA's plan basically puts a stamp on that.

## Why is GDA building it?

According to GDA officials, the township is a response to rising demand for organised housing in Gorakhpur. That makes sense. A lot of the city's recent growth has happened through private plotting, some approved and plenty not. A planned township gives buyers proper roads, drainage, layouts and legal clarity, which is what most families actually want.

The name "Gurukul" also hints at the idea of an education-friendly neighbourhood, with the township expected to benefit from nearby institutions as it grows.

GDA plans to develop it **in phases**, so don't expect all 400 acres to open at once.

## The bigger picture: Gorakhpur is on the move

Gurukul City isn't happening in isolation. A few things are pushing Gorakhpur's land market:

- **New Gorakhpur programme:** the state's push for planned expansion of the city, which Gurukul City is a part of.
- **Gorakhpur Link Expressway:** the 91 km link expressway has improved connectivity towards the Purvanchal Expressway and the rest of UP.
- **Industrial growth:** GIDA (Gorakhpur Industrial Development Authority) has been developing the Dhuriyapar Industrial Township, sometimes called "Greater Gida", with around 600 acres acquired across 17 villages for industrial use.
- **Other GDA schemes:** Raptinagar Vistar Township & Sports City in Manbela, and the long-running [Khorabar Township and Medicity project](/updates/2026-10-gda-khorabar-medicity-plots-auction-november-2026/).

When jobs, roads and planned housing all come together, land values usually follow. That's the pattern we've seen in Lucknow and Ayodhya, and Gorakhpur looks like it's in the early part of that curve.

## What should land buyers do right now?

As of 25 October 2026, there's no public registration for Gurukul City yet. So here's our honest advice:

**Don't pay anyone for "Gurukul City plots" right now.** Whenever a new township is announced, some people start selling land "inside" or "next to" the scheme before anything is official. Until GDA publishes the layout and opens registration, nobody can sell you a Gurukul City plot.

**Watch the surrounding area, carefully.** Land near a planned township often appreciates. But buying private land nearby only makes sense if the papers are clean and the land use allows what you want to build. Check the GDA master plan before buying anything on the corridor.

**Keep an eye on gdagkp.in.** That's where the official notice will appear.
