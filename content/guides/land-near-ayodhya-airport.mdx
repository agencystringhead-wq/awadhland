Half the calls I get about the airport are for land that cannot be sold, and half of the rest are for land that cannot be built on the way the buyer imagines. Here is the map as it stands, and what is actually on offer.

## Where the airport is, and what it took

Maharishi Valmiki International Airport sits south-west of the city, off the Lucknow highway. Its first terminal opened at the end of 2023. The expansion tracked on this site covers the next phase of terminal and runway works:

<ProjectCard id="ayodhya-airport-expansion" />

The airport's own land, including what was acquired for the expansion, is government land. It has a boundary, and it has fields and villages right up to that boundary. Everything inside is off the market for good. Everything outside is subject to two sets of limits before you can build.

## The two limits around an airport

**Height.** Around every airport the Airports Authority of India caps building height by zone, lowest along the runway line and rising with distance. Any construction near the airport needs an AAI no-objection certificate for height. A plot inside the funnel may be fine for parking or a single-storey warehouse and useless for a four-storey hotel.

**Land use.** The land beside the approach roads is mostly agricultural in the revenue record, whatever the hoardings say. The ADA master plan zones it; some of the airport side is marked for commercial and institutional use, some stays agricultural or green. A hotel needs commercial land use, which means the master plan zone must allow it and the plot must go through a Section 80 declaration and the authority's permission. The [conversion guide](/guides/agricultural-to-residential-conversion-up/) explains the process; the short version is that the zone decides, and no seller can promise it.

<Callout title="Two questions before any airport plot" tone="warn">
Is it outside the acquisition boundary, on the record? And what zone is it in on the master plan? Anyone selling airport-side land who cannot answer both with documents is selling the idea of the airport, not a plot.
</Callout>

## The approach roads

Airport Road and the stretch of the bypass towards it carry the traffic. Faizabad Road, further towards the city, is <Distance from="faizabad-road" to="airport" /> from the terminal by our measure and about 25 minutes by road; Civil Lines is <Distance from="civil-lines" to="airport" /> away. Closer to the terminal, the plots on offer are larger and mostly agricultural. The realistic uses, in the order the demand is arriving: parking and vehicle services, fuel, warehousing and logistics for the city's construction, budget lodging, then hotels on the few large plots with commercial land use and clean title. Retail follows the passenger numbers, which are still growing into the terminal's capacity.

## What a realistic deal looks like

- A plot outside the boundary, on the certified khatauni, in a zone the master plan marks for the use you want.
- A height cap you have checked against your plan, not against the seller's.
- The six [safety checks](/guides/how-to-check-if-land-in-up-is-safe-to-buy/) done, with particular attention to Gram Sabha land, which is common along the village edges here.
- A price that reflects that conversion and permissions are your cost and your time, unless the seller has already obtained them in writing.

## What is not a deal

Land inside the boundary. Land in a green or agricultural zone sold as "commercial". Plots in a "colony" with no RERA number. A share of a khasra sold as a whole. And any plot where the answer to "what can I build here" is "anything, sir".
