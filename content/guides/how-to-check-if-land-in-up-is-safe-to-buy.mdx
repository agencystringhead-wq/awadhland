Every plot I have seen go wrong went wrong at one of six checks, and every one of those checks costs less than a day. This is the order I do them in, before anyone pays a token, in Ayodhya, Lucknow or Gorakhpur alike.

<Checklist title="The six checks, in order">
- **Khatauni.** The seller's name, the exact khasra number, the area, and the land category.
- **Map.** The khasra on the village map matches the plot you walked.
- **Encumbrance.** No loan, mortgage, attachment or pending sale against it at the sub-registrar's office.
- **Mutation.** The last transfer is already recorded in the revenue record.
- **Land use.** What the master plan says the land is for.
- **RERA.** If it is a plotted development, the project's registration number and layout.
</Checklist>

## 1. Khatauni: who owns it, and what kind of land it is

The khatauni is the record of rights for a village. It is kept by the revenue department and it is what a court will look at first. Get it in two ways: the online copy from UP Bhulekh for a first look, and a certified copy from the tehsil for the file.

Read four things on it. The **name**: it must be the seller's, not a father's, a brother's or a company's, and if there are several names, every one of them has to sign. The **khasra number and area**: the plot you are being sold must be a khasra, or a defined share of one, and the area on the record must be the area in the agreement. The **category**: the column that says what kind of land it is. Words like Gram Sabha, banjar, charagah, talab, ceiling surplus or forest mean the land cannot be sold to you at all; the [separate guide on what you cannot buy](/guides/gram-sabha-ceiling-land-what-you-cannot-buy/) goes through each. And the **remarks**, where a pending case, a mortgage or a restriction on transfer sometimes shows up.

<Callout title="One rule" tone="warn">
If the name on the khatauni is not the name on the agreement, stop. Not after the token, before it. A power of attorney from the real owner can fix it, but only a registered one, and only if the owner is alive and consenting.
</Callout>

## 2. The map: the same plot on paper and on the ground

The khasra number is a number on the village map (the bhu-naksha). Pull the map, find the number, and walk the boundaries with the seller. Plots get sold from the wrong side of a road, from the neighbouring khasra, or as a whole when the seller holds a share. In Ayodhya this matters most on the roads that have been widened, because the record and the ground can differ by the width of the new road.

## 3. Encumbrance: what is registered against it

Every registered deed on a property is on file at the sub-registrar's office of that tehsil: sales, mortgages, agreements to sell, court attachments. A search for the last twelve to thirteen years shows what has been registered against the khasra. Ask for the search yourself or through a lawyer; do not rely on the seller's copy. IGRSUP's online index helps you find deeds by name and village, but the office search is the one to rely on.

What you are looking for is anything you were not told about: a loan still secured on the land, an earlier agreement to sell to someone else, a partition among heirs that was never completed.

## 4. Mutation: the record has caught up with the last sale

A registered sale deed transfers ownership. The revenue record does not update itself; the buyer applies for mutation (dakhil-kharij) at the tehsil, and the khatauni changes after notice and objections. Until that happens, the khatauni still shows the previous owner.

If your seller bought the plot and never mutated it, you are buying from someone the record does not know. Make the seller complete mutation before you sign. It is their application and their delay to bear, not yours.

## 5. Land use: what the plot is allowed to be

The khatauni says what the land is in the revenue record. The master plan says what the development authority will let you build. A plot can be recorded as agricultural and be inside the residential zone of the master plan, or be sold as residential and sit in a green belt. In Ayodhya the reference is the ADA master plan; in Lucknow, LDA's; in Gorakhpur, GDA's. Each locality page on this site lists the land use we found, with its source, for example Faizabad Road:

Agricultural land recorded as such needs a declaration under Section 80 of the UP Revenue Code before it can be used for a house. The [conversion guide](/guides/agricultural-to-residential-conversion-up/) covers who can apply and what it costs.

## 6. RERA: if it is a plotted development

A layout above 500 square metres or with more than eight plots needs UP RERA registration before it is marketed. Ask for the number, check it on the UP RERA site, and match the plot number to the approved layout. A "colony" without a number is being sold outside the law, however nice the gate.

## What safe looks like

Same name on the khatauni, the map and the agreement. A category that can be transferred. A clean search. Mutation done. Land use that matches what you want to build. A RERA number if there is a layout. When all six line up, the plot is as safe as a plot in UP gets; the rest is price.
